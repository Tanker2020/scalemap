Four designs from your last feedback: the contextual dock (config/cost/events scoped to the level you're on), the per-server config surface that replaces the bare selected-server card, a failover timeline you can actually read, and traffic placement on the globe that explains itself. Motion budget (≤8 strokes/view) and the price color carry over. No emojis anywhere — that's now app law.

THE LAW

1 · The contextual dock — one rule: scope = where you are + what you selected

The dock stops being a global wall. A new scope rail sits above the tabs — a breadcrumb of pills. Navigating sets the scope (enter an AZ → the dock is about that AZ); selecting narrows it further (tap a server on the floor → the dock is about that server). Any pill click widens back out; world is always one click. Tabs keep their names but their CONTENT is scoped: Cost is this server's burn, Events are its events, Analysis counts only findings touching it. Click the three buttons to feel the dock follow you.

WHY A RAIL, NOT A MODE

The scope rail is the dock's breadcrumb — always visible, always one click back to world. There is no "global vs local mode" to learn: the dock simply watches you. Enter, and it follows. Select, and it narrows. Widen whenever you want.

WHAT EACH TAB MEANS UNDER SCOPE

  • Config — the focused entity's own controls (replaces Topology below world scope: the world tree IS world-scope config).
  • Analysis — findings whose affected entities intersect the scope; the badge count follows.
  • Events — filtered to the scope's entity + its children.
  • Cost — the scope's own rollup, price-colored, with its egress share.
  • Blueprints / Traffic — world-scope only (they are inherently global); at narrower scopes they fold into the rail's world pill rather than showing global data in a local frame.

ONE STATE, TWO SURFACES

Floor/board selection and dock scope are the same store field. Selecting a pod scopes the dock; scoping via the rail highlights the entity in-scene. Nothing is configured "over there" anymore.

Cheap where it counts: every panel already reads the compiled world + nav focus — this is filtering and per-scope composition, not new stores. The one new store field: dockScope (kept in sync with nav + selection).

EVERY NUMBER EXPLAINS ITSELF

Spec knobs carry the consequence line ("8 cores → ~1,600 rps of db") computed from the resident blueprint's workload — the same math the traffic hero already uses. Change the knob, the sentence updates. No more silent specs.

WHERE IT LIVES

One component, two mounts: the dock's server-scope Config tab, AND (condensed: identity + rack + actions) the floor's selected-server card. Same dispatches everywhere — the relocated-dispatch contract holds.
READABLE

3 · The failover timeline — swimlanes + one sentence of narration

Today it's a cryptic glyph strip; this is a story. One lane per AZ, colored bands = that AZ's state over time (green fine, amber straining, red dead), labeled event markers with hover explanations, causality arrows connecting kill → detection → shift → promotion, and a narration line that says the latest chain in plain words. Time axis in sim-seconds, window = last 120 s, click any marker to scrub there.

What just happened: t=12s you killed us-east-1a → health checks failed 3× (t=15s) → traffic shifted to 1b (t=18s) → db-replica promoted (t=19s). Recovery holds until the outage clears.

Why this reads: the story runs left→right in real order, the bands make "who was dead when" visible without decoding anything, and the narration line means you never HAVE to read the lanes — it already told you. The dotted causality arrow links an event to its consequence in the other lane. All static except nothing — zero animation needed here; state is history, not flow.

INTUITIVE

4 · Placing traffic on the globe — a mode that explains itself

"+ traffic" enters placement mode: the cursor becomes a crosshair ghost that snaps to the nearest known city, a card previews who would send, how much, where it lands and at what latency — before you commit. Click places the population and opens its overlay with the rps slider; a ghost arc previews the route immediately, even with the sim stopped. Esc leaves the mode. No more hunting through the Traffic panel to make load exist.

us-east-1 eu-west-1
São Paulo · would send 500 rps
→ lands on us-east-1 · 142 ms · +$0.09/hr egress

The snap is the trick: free-clicking a globe is guesswork; snapping to the nearest real city (the same catalog population labels already use) makes every placement meaningful, and the preview card answers "what will this do" before the click. The solid arc is an existing population's live route; the faint dashed arc is the ghost preview. Placement is authoring → the mode button is edit-locked while the sim runs, like everything else now.
