Config UX, round 2 — where configuration lives ✓ decided: hybrid + hold-to-enter

Polish 1 restyled the panels; this round rethinks the model. Everything below uses your real world (the multi-region failover example) and the shipped kit language. Hover and click things — the interactions are live. Press-and-hold the green pin to feel the drill gesture.

Traffic is invisible

Incoming rps is the #1 thing to play with, and today it's buried three tabs deep behind a number field.

Config reads as a wall

Raw fields with no sentences around them — you can't tell what a number will do before you change it.

The scene is passive

You look at the globe but configure in a distant dock. Clicking the thing you're staring at should act on it.

Nothing responds

Almost no hover states or motion — the app feels inert, so it also feels harder than it is.

DIRECTION A

Command Overlays — configure in the scene

Click the thing you're looking at. Region pins, population dots, servers — one tap opens an anchored overlay with that entity's summary and its two or three controls that matter. Drilling in is a hold: press and hold a pin and a ring charges around it — when it completes, you're inside the region. Release early to cancel. Try it on the green pin. The dock shrinks to a world summary + entity list; deep editing stays in it, but you rarely need to go there.

DIRECTION B

Guided Console — the dock speaks sentences

Keep the dock, change its language. Every tab leads with a plain-English summary of what your world is doing, controls read as fill-in-the-blank sentences, and raw numbers hide behind expanders until you ask. Traffic gets a hero control at the top — impossible to miss.
